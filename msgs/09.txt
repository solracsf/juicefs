feat(meta): reserve an inode range and a hidden root for snapshots

Groundwork for snapshots; nothing allocates in the range or sets the flag
yet.

- SnapshotInode bounds the trash range from above and starts a range
  reserved for snapshot roots, with IsSnapshot(). The 39 IsTrash() call
  sites keep their meaning. The subTrash guard and the engines' test for
  a dated trash directory gain the upper bound, so a runaway nextTrash
  counter cannot hand out snapshot inodes and the first level of a
  snapshot is never taken for the trash.
- FlagSnapshot marks inodes that belong to a snapshot. mergeAttr refuses
  flag changes on them, root included: SetAttrFlag replaces the flag byte
  wholesale, so FlagImmutable alone could simply be cleared.
- .snapshots under the volume root resolves in Lookup to SnapshotInode,
  with no directory entry, like .trash. It is created on first use, so a
  volume that never snapshots carries nothing extra. The name cannot be
  created, linked, renamed or removed, and nothing can be made or cloned
  inside it by hand. isReservedEntry reserves both spellings of each
  hidden root, plain and with the .jfs prefix of --prefix-internal: a
  mount resolves only its own spelling, so an entry made under the other
  would shadow or double the hidden root. .snapshots is reserved at the
  root of the volume only, not at the root of a subdirectory mount; the
  trash keeps its checks at the root of every mount.
- Everything that finds the trash root by hand learns the new one too:
  the Redis leaked-inode sweep, GetPaths and GetParents, the dump
  loader's counters, BatchUnlink's guard, and Link, which would otherwise
  give frozen content a mutable path. A clone taken out of a snapshot
  drops the freeze.
- A load by a client unaware of the range counts snapshot inodes as
  trash and can leave the trash counter past the range. Each engine
  checks the counter in the transaction that creates an hourly trash
  directory and, when it is out of range, starts it over from the
  highest trash directory there is.
